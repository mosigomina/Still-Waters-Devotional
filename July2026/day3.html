July 3

You Are Strangers and Pilgrims on Earth

These all died in faith, not having received the promises, but having seen them afar off were assured of them, embraced them and confessed that they were strangers and pilgrims on the earth. (Hebrews 11:13)

The life of Abraham, the wandering man, did not begin in pilgrimage. Like everyone else, he had a country, relatives, and a father’s house. Then God called him to leave all three behind (Genesis 12:1).

From that day onward, the place Abraham came from became less important than the God who had called him. Even after building a family and spending decades in Canaan, Abraham still described himself as “a stranger and a sojourner” (Genesis 23:4). In other words, God’s call had given him a new identity and he had embraced that identity with everything he was and everything he had.

That same God has called everyone who belongs to Christ. We still live in earthly nations, we still cherish our people, and we still fulfil our responsibilities. But the One who called us has also given us a greater citizenship. And if the place of our birth explains where we came from, it must be God’s call that explains who we are and will become.

If we think of ourselves only as citizens of one or more earthly nations, we would live as if this world is all there is. But if we remember that we are strangers and pilgrims on the earth itself, then our decisions, desires, and loyalties will tell the same story Abraham’s life told. And you can be sure that it was a story that pleased the heart of God.

📖 Memory Verse

These all died in faith... and confessed that they were strangers and pilgrims on the earth.

(Hebrews 11:13)
🙏 Prayer

Dear God, thank You for calling me to Yourself. Help me to serve faithfully in the nation where You have placed me, while never forgetting where my true home is. Amen.
