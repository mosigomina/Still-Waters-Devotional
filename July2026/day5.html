July 5

You Are Ambassadors for Christ

Now then, we are ambassadors for Christ, as though God were pleading through us: we implore you on Christ’s behalf, be reconciled to God. (2 Corinthians 5:20)

During the 1924 Paris Olympics, Eric Liddell was Great Britain’s fastest sprinter and the heavy favourite to win gold in the 100-metre race. However, upon learning that the qualifying heats were scheduled for a Sunday, he withdrew from the race due to his convictions regarding the Sabbath. Years later, he gave up running altogether and returned to China (where he was born) as a missionary. He died there in 1945, in a Japanese internment camp, having spent his final years teaching, encouraging fellow prisoners, and giving away food rations to children and the weak.

While on the track, Liddell represented Britain. Everywhere else, this faithful man represented Christ. And when there was a clash between the first and the second, He chose Christ and continued to do so all the days of his life.

This is the picture behind 2 Corinthians 5:20. An ambassador does not invent their own message or pursue their own agenda. They carry the position, the authority, and the words of the one who sent them.

As believers in Christ, we are sent into the world the same way Christ was sent by the Father (John 17:18, 20:21). Every nation we belong to is, in this sense, a posting. We serve where we are sent, but the message we carry, and the One we represent, is never our own.

📖 Memory Verse

As You sent Me into the world, I also have sent them into the world.

(John 17:18)
🙏 Prayer

Dear God, let me carry Your message faithfully wherever I am sent. May my life plead Your case before the people I am placed among. Amen.
