July 29

We Look for a Heavenly Country

For here we have no continuing city, but we seek one to come. (Hebrews 13:14)

If you know that this world is not your home, will you stop caring about it? It is an old Christian debate. But let us look at what answer the Bible can lend to the discussion.

Hebrews 11:14–16 narrates that during the time of the patriarchs, they lived as strangers and pilgrims on the earth, seeking a country of their own. Notice what they did not do: withdraw from the cities they lived in, abandon their work, or treat their neighbours as insignificant or irrelevant people. More than anything, they longed for their true home (which is God’s house) without becoming passive in the land they currently inhabited.

It is not very different from us living many years after them, fortunate to have the cross of Christ in our past. And 2 Corinthians 5:1–2 gives it to us straight: that even our body is a tent we groan in, waiting for a permanent house. Thus, we groan. And as citizens of an earthly nation, we groan, aching to enter and reside in our heavenly nation.

And this is where the danger comes from, a danger than every believer in Christ must guard against. It is the danger of using Heaven as an excuse for earth. It is the danger of asking, “Since my citizenship is elsewhere, why build, why serve, why care?”

It sounds spiritual, for sure, but it is actually disengagement in disguise. From how the patriarchs lived, God expects us to live fully where He has planted us, that our hope of another country never becomes a reason to neglect this one.

So, is our citizenship in Heaven? Absolutely! Philippians 3:20 settles that question. Still, our duty to this nation remains as long as we are here. We serve our nation as people who are not desperate for it to give us everything, because it was never meant to. We hold it loosely, serve it faithfully, and let our eyes stay fixed on the country still to come.

📖 Memory Verse

But now they desire a better, that is, a heavenly country.

(Hebrews 11:16)
🙏 Prayer

Dear God, let my longing for Your country never excuse me from faithfulness in this one. Teach me to hold what is temporary with open, working hands. Amen.
