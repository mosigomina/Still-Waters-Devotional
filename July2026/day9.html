July 9

Daniel Stood in the Gap for His Nation

So I set my face toward the Lord my God to seek Him by prayer and supplications, with fasting, sackcloth, and ashes. (Daniel 9:3)

When Daniel decided to pray, it came on the heels of Jeremiah’s declaration decades earlier that the exile would last seventy years and then end. By Daniel’s own calculation, that number was something God had committed in writing, and it was nearly up.

So why pray at all? If the ending was already settled, what was left for prayer to accomplish?

This is the question every person who has prayed for a nation eventually runs into, in one form or another. We pray for change, for years, sometimes for a lifetime, and wonder whether the prayer is doing anything that wasn’t already going to happen, or that isn’t simply never going to happen. Daniel had the opposite problem and prayed anyway. He had every reason to assume the outcome was secure, and he still gave himself to fasting, sackcloth, and an extended confession that runs sixteen verses long.

What Daniel seemed to understand is that prayer was never primarily about producing an outcome. It was the way he placed himself rightly inside a promise that was already moving toward its fulfilment. His prayer talked him into the posture a person needs to carry when they are about to receive something they did not personally earn.

This is what it means to stand in the gap. It is not always because something is broken and needs to be fixed through enough prayer. Sometimes, it is that someone needs to be present, humbled, and accountable for a people while God’s word does what it already said it would do.

📖 Memory Verse

We do not present our supplications before You because of our righteous deeds, but because of Your great mercies.

(Daniel 9:18)
🙏 Prayer

Dear God, let me come before You for this nation not because I doubt what You have promised, but because I want to stand rightly inside it. Amen.
