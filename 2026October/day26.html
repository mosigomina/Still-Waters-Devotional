OCTOBER 26

Maximising God’s Grace

And He said to me, “My grace is sufficient for you, for My strength is made perfect in weakness.” Therefore most gladly I will rather boast in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me. Therefore I take pleasure in infirmities, in reproaches, in needs, in persecutions, in distresses, for Christ’s sake. For when I am weak, then I am strong. (2 Corinthians 12:9–10)

Grace refers to a gift or blessing, especially one that is unmerited. Among the many graces that God has bestowed on mankind, there is none greater, none more impactful, none as eternally glorious as the way of salvation which He achieved through the Lord Jesus Christ. And as for all who have received this gift and blessing and stepped into this salvation, grace continues its work in their lives.

There are various ways in which God unfolds His grace in the life of a believer. Because of God’s compassion, we were given a way out of the life of sin and its consequences (notably death and destruction). Digging deeper in our walk with Him, we see that our acceptance and laying hold of what He has done for us leads us to becoming children of God. And it is because we are children that we are open to being trained and disciplined into growth.

The concept of being taught by grace as used in Titus 2:11–13 is similar to traditional views of training a child. So, by grace we have been saved, so we may be taught how to be and how to live, to the end that we are perfect just as our Heavenly Father is perfect.

In 1 Corinthians 15:9–10, a practical example of a man who was saved and taught by grace is given. Apostle Paul, the man in question, went from persecuting to renouncing that way of persecuting in favour of labouring over what he persecuted men for. This is grace in action.

Indeed, grace unfolds itself as God’s help. It can be strength in weak times or your steps being ordered by the Lord. God’s grace is enough help for you, granted that you act upon the grace you received. This is why Apostle Paul said that the grace of God to him was not in vain (1 Corinthians 15:10).

That grace did not make the apostle lazy. On the contrary, it taught him to labour abundantly. There’s a lesson in there for you. And just in case you are not always feeling ‘engraced’ in this regard, the Word of God is weightier than your feelings and assumptions.
