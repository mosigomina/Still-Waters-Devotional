OCTOBER 25

Discipleship

The things which you learned and received and heard and saw in me, these do, and the God of peace will be with you. (Philippians 4:9)

Discipleship is the process that results in us becoming more like Jesus as we commit ourselves to His Word and to His ways. It is not just about learning but also doing.

In 2 Timothy 2:2, we get an insight into the form that discipleship takes. Paul tells Timothy to commit to others what has been committed to him [the same things he had observed from Paul]. The Amplified Bible translation of ‘commit’ there is ‘transmit.’ The end result of such a transmission is that those who received this deposit are transformed according to what has been deposited into them.

The transformation in question is weak or strong depending on how you received the deposit. How you received the deposit is a function of your committing to the process of discipleship. After all, discipleship is a depositing (giving) and a committing (receiving) process for a goal. And although the impact of the depositing aspect is most clearly seen in how God uses you for an assignment, the biggest impact is in you.

Jesus deposited into the disciples in order to make them effective witnesses. But this did not only help them do an assignment; it further improved the quality of who they were as followers. A notable example is Peter, who went from being self-confident and self-preserving to being someone who knew and lived the message of the cross.

Your commitment to those the Lord sends you to disciple isn’t so they can worship you. It is so you can present an easy example for them to follow, with the ultimate aim that they will follow and become like Christ. Indeed, discipleship is not embodying the personalities of other believers; it is following their examples of obedience, love, longsuffering and the many other virtues of Christ that are clearly at work in their lives.

(Philippians 4:9)
