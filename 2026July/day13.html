July 13

Rebelling Against Authority is Rebelling Against God

Whoever resists the authority resists the ordinance of God, and they that resist shall receive to themselves judgment. (Romans 13:2)

When we think of rebelling against authority, rarely do some of us think about the strange story that Jude drops in his epistle. According to him, Michael the archangel, disputing directly with the devil over the body of Moses, refused to bring even a railing accusation against him; he simply said, the Lord rebuke you, and left the verdict entirely in God’s hands.

Consider what is happening here. Michael was not arguing with a human government or an earthly ruler. He was face to face with the devil himself, in a dispute where he had every reason and every right to unleash the full weight of his authority. And he restrained himself anyway, leaving judgment to the One who actually holds it.

Jude brings this up to expose people who speak evil of dignities with a casualness an archangel would never permit himself. They do, in his words, what even Michael refused to do. So, when we read Romans 13:2, we are right to interpret it literally: that resisting an authority is the same as resisting an ordinance, something established and owned by God, not by the person currently occupying the position.

Here again, we must call up Titus 3:1-2 as we did yesterday. Even in daily speech, be ready to do every good work, speaking evil of no one. The restraint Michael showed toward the devil is the same restraint we are asked to show toward authorities far less menacing than he was.

📖 Memory Verse

Yet Michael the archangel... durst not bring against him a railing accusation, but said, The Lord rebuke thee.

(Jude 1:9)
🙏 Prayer

Dear God, give me the same restraint Michael showed, leaving judgment over those in authority in Your hands rather than my mouth. Amen.
