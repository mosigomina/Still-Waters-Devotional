July 10

The Effective Prayer of a Righteous Person

The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much. (James 5:16)

When the apostle James wanted to back up the statement that forms our Bible reference, he reached immediately for an incredulous example: Elijah. Elijah, the man who, in his days, closed the sky for three and a half years through prayer. Then, James, by the Spirit, calls Elijah a man with a nature like ours.

Wow! It is easy to read about a prophet who altered the weather of an entire nation and quietly assume he belonged to a different category of believer, someone with a direct line to heaven that ordinary people simply lack. James removes that assumption directly.

If Elijah has the same nature we have, it means he carried similar passions, similar struggles, similar human limitations. So what does “righteous” mean here, if Elijah was so ordinary?

The Bible teaches that it points to a life rightly aligned with God, sincere in its dealings with Him, the kind 2 Chronicles 7:14 describes as people who humble themselves and turn from their own ways before they ever bring a request. In other words, the reason the prayer carries power is that something has been set right between that person and God first.

This dismantles the most common excuse for hesitant prayer over our nation. God is not waiting for someone holier, more equipped, more spiritually advanced than we feel ourselves to be. Elijah was a man like us, and, because of his relationship with God, moved heaven anyway.

📖 Memory Verse

Elijah was a man with a nature like ours, and he prayed earnestly... and the earth produced its crops.

(James 5:17-18, paraphrased)
🙏 Prayer

Dear God, give me the confidence to pray boldly for this nation. Make my life right before You, and let that be enough. Amen.
