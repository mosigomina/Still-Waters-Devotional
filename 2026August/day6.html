AUGUST 6

The Test of Trust

And you shall remember that the Lord your God led you all the way these forty years in the wilderness, to humble you and test you, to know what was in your heart, whether you would keep His commandments or not. So He humbled you, allowed you to hunger, and fed you with manna which you did not know nor did your fathers know, that He might make you know that man shall not live by bread alone; but man lives by every word that proceeds from the mouth of the Lord. (Deuteronomy 8:2–3)

There are seasons in our lives when it feels like nothing is happening at all, and we’re stagnant. We pray, but the answer seems delayed. We wait for a breakthrough, yet the situation remains the same. During these moments, it is very easy to think that God has forgotten us or that He is doing nothing. The Bible shows us that God often uses the waiting season to build something greater within us.

Sometimes, God allows us to go through seasons where we cannot depend on our own strength or wisdom. It is during these moments that we discover that He is enough. Waiting is His way of teaching us to trust Him more than ever.

John 6:5–6 teaches us that God sometimes allows us to face certain situations to strengthen our faith. The test is not because God does not know us; He already knows our hearts. The test helps us discover whether our confidence is truly in Him or in our own understanding.

Waiting seasons often reveal who we are. They show whether we will continue to trust God when we cannot see immediate results. They teach us patience, dependence, perseverance, and obedience. While we are waiting for God to change our circumstances, He is often changing us, for a better walk with Him.

📖 MEMORY VERSE
And you shall remember that the Lord your God led you all the way these forty years in the wilderness, to humble you and test you, to know what was in your heart.
(Deuteronomy 8:2)
🙏 PRAYER
Lord, teach me to remain faithful while I wait on You, knowing that You are using every season to increase my faith in You.
