AUGUST 25

Rebuilding the Ruins

“Come, let us rebuild the wall of Jerusalem, that we may no longer be a reproach.” (Nehemiah 2:17)

The saying “actions have consequences” is true. The apostle Paul wrote that whatever a man sows, he shall reap. Most times the ruins in our lives are the result of our disobedience, neglect, and wrong choices. My intention by the end of this reading is for you to see the sovereignty of God in both reversible and irreversible consequences.

The Lord told Israel while they were in the wilderness that if they failed to obey Him and follow all His commands in the land they were about to possess, the land itself would vomit them out (Deuteronomy 28:63–64). This is quite like a stone that has been set rolling, stopping only when we stop it. Put differently, we can reverse the consequences by choosing to repent and obey God.

But there are also those consequences that are hard to reverse, requiring God’s intervention for anything to be done about it. For example, God’s judgment on Moses for striking the rock when he had been instructed to speak to it (Numbers 20:12). Moses prayed and begged but God told him not to speak to Him about it again (Deuteronomy 3:26).

Or the case of David arranging for the death of Uriah to preserve his name. Here again, God passed judgment that the sword would not leave David’s household (2 Samuel 12:9–10). Did God forgive the man? Yes. But was the judgment reversed? No.

The point here is that whether in reversible and irreversible, God is the constant. It is He that passes judgment. He is the same One that overrides the judgment if He so chooses.

Knowing this, we constantly appeal to Him and to His mercy. But thank God that God is merciful and loving, slow to anger and full of constant love (Psalm 103:8). And even with seemingly irreversible consequences to our choices, decisions, and actions, God is able to reverse and restore.
