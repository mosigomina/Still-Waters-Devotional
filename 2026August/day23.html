AUGUST 23

The Grief of Wandering

It is of the Lord’s mercies that we are not consumed, because His compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is Thy faithfulness. (Lamentations 3:22–23)

The deepest grief of wandering comes from failing God repeatedly. It is returning to the path you promised never to walk again, picking up the habit you vowed to drop, and committing the sin you swore to abandon.

Repeated failure breeds shame. Shame convinces us to run, hide, and stay as far from God as possible. The guilt that ought to drive us back to Him ends up keeping us away.

The prodigal son travelled far from home and threw his life away. When he finally hit rock bottom, he carefully rehearsed his speech: Father, I have sinned against both heaven and you. I am no longer worthy to be called your son. Just make me one of your hired hands. He braced himself for cold rejection, a seat in the servants’ quarters, or a shut door.

Instead, while he was still a long way off, his father spotted him. Moved with deep compassion, the father ran to him, wrapped his arms around his neck, and kissed him.

God’s mercies renew every morning, and His compassion never runs dry. He meets us at the place of mercy, and that ground remains firm no matter how many times we return to it. Paul asks in Romans 6:1 whether we should keep sinning so grace can abound. The very necessity of that question shows just how vast His grace actually is.

📖 MEMORY VERSE
“His compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is Thy faithfulness.”
(Lamentations 3:22–23)
🙏 PRAYER
Lord, I have wandered again. I am coming home today because I know You are running to meet me. Receive me as Your mercy allows. Amen.
