NOVEMBER 7

The Adjusting Heart

Therefore I will always remind you about these things, even though you know them and are established in the truth you now have. (2 Peter 1:12)

In today’s Bible reference, the Apostle Peter explains that his reason for writing the epistle is to “establish” truth in the hearts of believers. The word is active and communicates an intention to dwell, not just to visit. And because truth does not bend, anywhere it stays usually has to change. This is why the believer is described as an adjusting person.

The Lord Jesus is not just a teacher of the truth or a teacher of truths. He is the Truth (John 14:6). You have to bear this in mind when you read that the truth requires you to adjust to it. And adjusting to the truth means being yieldable, surrendering to God a heart that is soft, willing to learn, open to correction.

So, it is not endurance alone that you should work on as you look to finishing strong. You also need to work on responsiveness, to keep your heart teachable. When the truth of God’s Word confronts you, do not resist it. Consider it and overlay it on all you knew previously, weighing its implications, and determining in your heart to love and practise it.

Back to where we started: Peter wrote repeatedly, not because the people did not know, but because the human heart needs to be constantly recalibrated. If it stays uncorrected, it could soon go off course.
