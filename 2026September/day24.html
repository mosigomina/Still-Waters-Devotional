SEPTEMBER 24

Community and Desire

“They go from strength to strength, every one of them appears before God in Zion.” (Psalm 84:7)

Among the 27 books of the New Testament, it can be argued that based on dedication, seven (including the Gospel according to Luke and Acts) were written to individuals and the remaining 20 to groups. It is a bit of a stretch, but we can settle on that to say that for every Gospel or Epistle to an individual, God had three for groups.

If grouping and community are this important to God, is it possible that even our desire and love for Him could be influenced, maybe even determined, because of community? The Bible answers, yes!

A Christian community can affect our desire for God; the clearest evidence of this is in Proverbs 27:17—as iron sharpens iron, so does one person sharpen another. In everyday life and walk with God, we find it easier to trust God when we find others doing so. And courage comes quicker from company than when we are alone. The same goes for encouragement when we expect God to do one thing and He does something else.

But a Christian community can also wear down and wear out our love for God, usually when we define and impose the specifics of the relationships of others with God on our relationship with Him (Galatians 2:11–13). When they fail, we find ourselves wondering if we also will. When they succeed, we might compare ourselves with them.

Ultimately, there is no running alone. As long as we hope to love God until the end, we must have others by our side doing the same. Then, the walk will be easier.
