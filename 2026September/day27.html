SEPTEMBER 27

Growing Desire

“When they had finished eating, Jesus asked Simon Peter, ‘Simon son of John, do you love Me more than these?’ ‘Yes, Lord,’ he answered, ‘You know I love You.’” (John 21:15)

Much have we said about desiring God, including its different dynamics. If we have not pounded on this fact loudly enough, here it is in plain language: no true loss comes from desiring and loving God. To the person interested in a relationship with the Maker of the heavens and the earth, redeemer of men, and judge of all things, loving God is a lifeline we cannot aggrandise.

Focusing today on the relationship between desiring God and spiritual maturity, we can define the latter as growing ever closer to God’s intended spiritual condition for all people. It is, for us on the other side of the Lord’s death and resurrection, a matter of maximising the endowments of Christ’s salvation in an individual’s life and living, cares and character, and everything else that concerns them.

For you, believer in Christ, to desire God is to desire to be closer to Him; to see Him, not through the blurring bifocals of assumptions, but through faith; to know Him as well as He knows you. If you don’t continue to desire Him, from where will the desire come to search the Scriptures to know what He has said and done?

It is as the apostle Peter said: it is when we crave the sincere milk of God’s Word that we can grow in the way of salvation (1 Peter 2:2). It is when we crave God’s life and truth and ways that we inch ever closer to Him, quick to cast aside all that distract and disrupt.

To reiterate, no true loss comes from desiring God. Desiring God makes it easier to grow in the way of salvation. Growing in the way of salvation makes it easier to know God, grow closer to Him, and enjoy Him.
