SEPTEMBER 8

The Sufficiency of God

“But she said to them, ‘Do not call me Naomi; call me Mara, for the Almighty has dealt very bitterly with me. I went out full, and the Lord has brought me home again empty. Why do you call me Naomi, since the Lord has testified against me, and the Almighty has afflicted me?’” (Ruth 1:20–21)

It’s easy to love God when life is going well. However, our love often gets tested when everything falls apart.

In Ruth 1:20–21, Naomi is grieving deep loss and feels the bitterness of life. But even in that situation, God is shown clearly working in the background, delivering to Naomi a daughter-in-law (nay, a son) of inestimable worth.

Habakkuk takes this even further in Habakkuk 3:17–18. The picture he paints is one of total disaster: the crops fail, the orchards die, and there is no food anywhere. By all logic, he has every reason to panic or give up. Instead, he makes an incredible choice: “Yet I will rejoice in the Lord, I will be joyful in God my Savior.” He realises that wasn’t his real source of life; God was.

On the flip side, Jeremiah 17:5–6 warns us about trusting human strength or looking everywhere else for security, leaving us dry and empty like a desert weed.

To realise that God is enough is to let go of treating Him like a vending machine for the things we actually want, like comfort or easy answers. Instead, He becomes the treasure we hold true.

📖 MEMORY VERSE
Though the fig tree may not blossom, nor fruit be on the vines; though the labour of the olive may fail, and the fields yield no food; though the flock may be cut off from the fold, and there be no herd in the stalls—yet I will rejoice in the Lord, I will joy in the God of my salvation.
(Habakkuk 3:17–18)
🙏 PRAYER
In the midst of every other option, help me to choose You over again. Amen!
